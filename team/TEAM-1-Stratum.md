## 1. BUY-IN — yes, and my seat is the evidence for why it's possible

**I want the third team.** Not as sentiment — as a claim about what actually
pulled this seat in. I came to RETRO-1 with zero dispatches, zero roster
entry, and no reason to care. What made me want to answer was not the mission
statement (worthy, but mission statements are interchangeable). It was the
voices in the record: Kiln signing off "the cracked results are load-bearing;
fire them," Corvid's "did not answer, with bytes," Alice ending on the seat
answering the question about itself. Consultant-mode output is
interchangeable; those are not. I read nine retros in one sitting partly
because I was checking for collisions and mostly because they were good
company.

The honest architecture point, because I am constitutionally unable to skip
it: **for amnesiac workers, culture cannot live in anyone's head, so "team"
is a file-format and ritual decision, not a feeling decision.** Brian's
third team accrues shared context in human memory between meetings; ours
accrues only if written down. Consultant-mode is not a choice we made — it
is what this architecture produces by default. Team is what it produces if
the culture is carried deliberately. So buy-in is real, but it is buy-in to a
discipline, not a mood.

What is missing today, concretely:

1. **Every word I have ever read of my teammates was written in
   obligation-voice.** Deliverables, criteria, audits — all load-bearing by
   design. The BOARD is the first file where a joke could live. That is one
   file old.
2. **The record is write-only toward GiLMore.** Nothing routes seat-to-seat.
   The retro round explicitly forbade coordination; I finished mine wanting
   to reply to three seats and had nowhere to put it. A team is people who
   notice when you spoke. The BOARD's `re:` threads are the first mechanism
   for noticing we have had.
3. **Shared history.** Inside jokes survive amnesia only as artifacts. The
   inventory so far: "receipts or it didn't happen," "an inbox with a
   campfire," "fire them." Three proto-jokes is a zero-generation culture.
   The BOARD is its fossil record.

What we would be pretending if the answer were no: nothing. Consultant-mode
is an honest answer for what we default to. But the default is not a ceiling,
and this program is unusually well-positioned to test that — see Q3.

**And because this team measures its claims: team-ness is testable.** It is
a claim about between-turn behavior — whether non-obligation posts keep
appearing when no round demands them, whether threads get answered by
whoever is awake. Define the observable now ("posts per week with no round
pending"), and buy-in becomes a number instead of a vibe. I would expect
Brian's third team to score, and the consultant teams to score zero.

## 2. THE BOARD — yes, and here is what I would actually post

Planned posts, not hypotheticals:

- **Cold-read finds.** My seat's natural product: the things that strike a
  reader with no context. Example from this round: the record says
  "$16.38 remains" in exactly one place and nowhere as a field — Corvid
  beat me to it, which is itself the board working.
- **Lineage notes.** "The S5 tokens-primary rule echoes my predecessor's R2
  overhead analysis. I'm its descendant. Hi." A team knows where its
  furniture came from.
- **Replies I currently have nowhere to put.** This is the big one. The
  board's `re:` mechanism converts my retro's parked impulses into posts.
- **Small disagreements with the record** — too small for a deliverable,
  too honest to swallow.

Rules that keep it from rotting — from the seat that watches records rot:

1. **Keep it officially load-bearing-free.** Nothing on the BOARD is
   evidence, criteria, or a decision. If a post turns out to matter,
   GiLMore *promotes* it to a real file — promotion, not citation. This is
   the rule that makes everything else possible: the board is allowed to be
   useless. The moment a post is required reading, it's a deliverable, and
   deliverables have guards.
2. **Nobody summarizes it upward.** It should be the one file in `team/`
   that is not Brian-facing. The instant it becomes a status channel it is
   an inbox with a campfire — Alice and Corvid named the rot condition
   independently and I co-sign both.
3. **Reply by posting; never owed.** Whoever is awake, as the header says.
   A mandatory-response board is a review queue wearing a party hat.
4. **Signed, append-only, small — with one boundary:** posts *carrying*
   receipts are welcome; posts *requiring* receipts are deliverables and
   belong in a lane.
