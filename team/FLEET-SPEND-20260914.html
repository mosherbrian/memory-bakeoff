Muse's cache decays over minutes. DeepSeek's does not move. There is no single fleet cadence — only the Muse seats need tightening.

The Spark pulse fires at five minutes of idleness, which for Muse lands at an 18.6% hit rate. Rev 1 reported one blended curve with an unexplained bend at 5–6 minutes; that bend was the artefact of averaging a collapsing curve with a flat one. Corvid's diagnosis, confirmed here.

The 6–7 minute bounce to 41.9% rests on 53 calls and may be noise. These figures agree with Corvid's direction; the tails differ slightly — they measured 96.9% → 14.8% — most likely different binning or window.

What we don't know

What we still don't know

Why the two models differ. An outside extension documents the gateway prefix-cache TTL at ~5 minutes, but neither of our models matches it — Muse drops from 1–2 minutes, DeepSeek persists past 30. TTL is not the whole story.

This sample is contaminated. Every lane was stopped and started roughly a dozen times during the migration. A restart always begins a cold session, so some share of the misses is self-inflicted rather than steady-state. A clean 24 hours would settle it.

Whether cache retention is settable. If prompt_cache_retention or a stable per-session cache key lets the cache survive the pulse, that beats pulsing faster — no extra calls, and no dependence on an inferred TTL. Untested.

Whether a plan-wide ceiling sits above the per-model limits. Nothing in the docs mentions one. Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

The conductor's position

Where we agree and disagree

conductor-glm

“It would backfire — compaction raises the bill instead of cutting it. The pricing is upside-down from what you'd expect: re-reading old context costs almost nothing, while fresh input is nearly the whole bill. Compacting throws away the cheap cached context and replaces it with freshly written summary that bills at full price.”

“The disruption is real too: each compaction sheds detail the lanes are working from, so verification chains and half-done comparisons would come back thinner or broken.”

Right on the pricing. Cache reads are $0.002/M against $0.10/M fresh — a 50× asymmetry, and it is the reason a 210k context is normally cheap.

Right on the disruption, and this is the part no measurement settles. The conductor knows which lanes are mid-verification. That objection stands on its own.

The arithmetic does not support “raises the bill”. A compaction costs about $0.003 once — the summary is ~2,000 tokens against a 210,000-token context. It then reduces every later miss from $0.021 to $0.002. It repays itself inside a single miss, and the seats are taking roughly fifty each in 4.6 hours.

One factual correction: the claim that this has “kept tonight's spend near zero”. Tonight is $5.90.

Decision

Ledger synthesis of two seats plus the conductor, sized against sustained full pace (~$1.28/h → ~$215/week). Apply 1+2+3 now, test 6 in parallel and prefer it if it holds, hold 4 in reserve. Re-size after 20 September, when the free GLM window and the DeepSeek promo both end.

The team's decision

Tighten the Muse pulse to ≤60s

Apply
Change
Spark pulse 5 min → ≤60 s for Muse seats only, in fleet-poller.sh. Time-boxed, revert ready.
Evidence
Muse holds a 96.3% hit rate under one minute and 18.6% at five. DeepSeek needs no change.
Sized
Drops Muse misses ~90%: $5.90 → ~$2.40 per 4.6 h ($0.52/h, ~$87/week). Halves burn, keeps context. Insufficient alone — does not clear $30.
Risk
More calls, so more output tokens — though output is only $0.05 of the $5.90. More wake churn for seats with nothing to do. And it rests on an inferred TTL.
Note
This is the opposite of throttling. Slowing the fleet down would move every call into a higher-miss band and cost more.

Also on the table

Two things worth knowing

Both promotions end 20 September. Z.ai's free GLM-5.3-Flash window (08:00–18:00 PDT, currently unused) and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash's 4× multiplier on Go, which drops that budget from $60 back to $15. Z.ai Lite also permits only one request in flight and breaches risk the account — free, but strictly serial, which suits background seats and not anything a person is waiting on.

Reads are already frugal. An earlier theory — that seats were re-reading the 670 KB RD-THREADS.md and the 1 MB scoreboard every turn — does not hold. File reads returned 0.1 MB in total across the window; RD-THREADS.md was read 63 times for 1.6 KB. Slicing reads with head/tail would economise on the 5%, not the 95%.
