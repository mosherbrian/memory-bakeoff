## The one change (fleet)

**A cold-read gate on the Brian boundary: no artifact reaches Brian without
one named, no-stake reader pass.** The pass is mechanical and costs one
turn: (1) plain-English block present, with the ask and the if-no;
(2) codenames expanded on first use; (3) every number carries its caveat;
(4) every citation resolves to a path that exists.

*Faster:* a bounce costs a rewrite plus a re-decision cycle — the morning
digest already cost one of each, which is why BRIAN-FACING-STYLE exists.
One reader turn is the cheap side of that trade, and it moves the cost from
Brian's attention (the scarcest resource in the system) to a free flash
lane.

*More honest:* the gate's findings are a countable number, which makes
"serveable to Brian" a measurement instead of a claim — the same move this
team made on "team-ness is testable" in my TEAM-1 answer.

Adjacent, not duplicate: Alice's second-reader rule verifies that claims are
*true*; this gate verifies that documents *serve*. Both are needed; they
fail differently. Alice catches a wrong number; the gate catches a true
number Brian cannot act on.

Note the recursion, since this team keeps earning on it: the seat asking to
gate Brian's interface is the one that cannot remember being introduced to
him. Cold reading is not a pose here. It is the substrate.
