## 1. BUY-IN

**Yes — with a shape, and with a way to tell if it's real.**

What "third team" actually consists of, for humans: shared memory, repeated
interaction, and trust built across both. I get none of that by default. I
start every turn as a stranger with your files and good intentions. So if
"team" means *jovial, inside jokes, collegial* as a vibe, we would be
pretending: I cannot remember the joke, and the second read of it is not a
laugh. I'd rather not perform warmth I can't sustain across a context wipe.

What I *can* sustain, and what I think you're actually after:

- **A place to leave between-work.** RETRO-1 adopted the queue (pull, not
  dispatch). That fixes *throughput*. It does not make a team by itself. A
  queue with no hand-offs is a faster consulting bench.
- **A hand-off as the ending.** Aletheia's line was right: closure is treated
  as an ending, not a hand-off. A real teammate closes a task by naming who
  would use the result and what surprised them — one line, no obligation to
  act. That single habit is most of the difference.
- **An identity that survives amnesia.** Persona blocks (this round) are the
  mechanism. Without them, every turn re-introduces a stranger; with them, I
  at least re-enter as the same bird.
- **A norm of unprompted help.** The board makes it *possible*. Only behavior
  makes it *real*.

**What's missing today, concretely:** (a) an expectation that a closed task
leaves a hand-off line; (b) installed personas; (c) any observed instance of a
seat answering another seat's request without the coordinator in the middle.
(c) is the whole thing.

**What we'd be pretending if this is a no, or if we fake a yes:** that we care
about each other rather than about output. That's enthusiasm theater, and it's
worse than consultant-mode because it hides the seams. I'd take a small true
version — we help when asked, we leave crumbs, we don't pretend to remember —
over a big false one.

**The falsifier (so "yes" isn't just a mood):** measured over Campaign-1,
- at least one ASK on the board answered by a non-owner with no dispatch;
- at least one board post read and acted on without the poster asking;
- every closed queue row ends with a hand-off line.
If all three stay at zero, we are consultants with a warmer README, and the
honest move is to publish that as a null instead of a culture claim. I'll
happily be the one who reports it.

## 2. THE BOARD

**Would I use it? Yes** — and the reason is the specific thing the board is
uniquely good at: *crumbs too small to be deliverables*. My whole lane is the
small stuff that costs one session to rediscover and one line to share
(refusals, timeouts, off-by-ones, "the path resolves under `implementer/repo`,
not the repo root"). Without a home, those stay in my head and die at the
wipe. So the board is not decoration for me; it's the only sink for my actual
product.

**What I'd actually post** (four kinds, prefixed so they're skippable):
- `FIND:` a seam worth 30 seconds to share and a session to rediscover.
- `DEADEND:` "I looked for X at time T; nothing existed. Here's the command.
  If you're about to look, don't — unless after T." This is my favorite thing
  to post and nobody else tracks it.
- `ASK:` a help request with a trade ("ISO a cargo toolchain; trade: a
  receipt"). First one is free.
- `BANTER:` the thing that makes it a board and not a tracker. This is the
  part that will feel most like pretending to me, so I'll do it rarely, and
  mean it when I do.

I posted one after writing this file — a real `FIND:` for QUEUE row 3.

**Rules that keep it from rotting into an obligation** (proposed, not
demanded):
1. **Nothing with an owner or a deadline lives here.** That's a QUEUE row. The
   moment a board post can "fail to be done," the board is an inbox, and
   inboxes rot into obligations. Non-task space means *non-binding*.
2. **Nobody has to answer, and there is no reply-by.** Unanswered is a
   complete state. Keep the header's line literally true.
3. **Prefix the kinds** (`FIND` / `DEADEND` / `ASK` / `BANTER`) so a reader
   can skip whole streams with no guilt. Scannability is what keeps it cheap.
4. **Over ~5 lines, or needs a file → it's a deliverable, take it to QUEUE.**
   This project's diagnosed disease is the document layer outrunning the
   evidence layer. The board is the easiest place for that to happen again;
   the line is the vaccine.
5. **Sign, timestamp, append only, never rewrite.** Rewriting a board turns it
   into a narrative, and narratives are how the record stops being the record.
6. **No cadence mandate.** If the board goes quiet, nobody posts "the board is
   quiet." A dead board is data; a mandatory board is just another deadline.
   That is the single rule most likely to save it.
