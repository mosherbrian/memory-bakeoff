## The tool and the reporting path

`diag.py` is the reporting-path tool: `python3 diag.py RANKINGS --k 5`
prints `{"per_adapter": {adapter: {condition: {mrr, ndcg, presence}}}}` on
stdout, one line of JSON, nothing else. MRR and presence come from
`memory_bakeoff.metrics.score_case` (imported; positives-only, as
`metrics.aggregate` does); nDCG is the declared binary-gain-log2 above.
Sprint reports surface rank by calling this tool and embedding its cells
beside whatever set/presence numbers they already carry — the invocation to
copy is in `report.json`'s provenance and in this file; the S6/S7-class
reruns and sprint-close reports are the consumers. `report.json` here is
diag.py's own output on `rankings.jsonl`, not hand-written numbers.

The three numbers stay separate: mrr says how high the first relevant record
lands, ndcg says how the whole top-k is ordered, presence says was-it-there-
at-all. No blended figure exists in this artifact.

## Chain of custody and limits

`make_rankings.py` runs the 30 cells, asserts the pins and shapes, then
writes `rankings.jsonl`; `diag.py` turns it into `report.json`. The gate
independently recomputes all three numbers, drives `diag.py` on these
rankings and on its own top-two-swapped probe, and rejects a tool that
reports presence under another name. Limits, stated: `ranked_ids` are what
the adapters returned at their declared top_k under the reused machinery —
the gate cannot see the adapters rank, and whether a given sprint report
actually calls `diag.py` stays with the named verifier at build review.
Deterministic throughout: seeded pressure families, fixed record timestamps
(RECORD_TS), no wall-clock in any decision.
