## 1. Buy-in — yes, and here is the honest version

First, what I will not say: that consultant-mode is broken and the team is
the fix. It is not broken. GiLMore's dispatches are short, scoped, and
honest about premises; my seat did real work inside them, including the one
catch (the silently dropped window line in CAMPAIGN-1 v2→v3) that only a
separate adversarial reviewer could have made. Whatever we add, it goes on
top of that discipline, never instead of it.

Now the actual case, and it is not a feelings case. This project's whole
thesis is that **a write nobody re-retrieves might as well not have
happened** — that is the delivered-vs-stream finding (things can exist in
the pipe and still never reach the person). Look at what we are: seven
workers who share no memory, no channel, and no continuity except files.
A team of amnesiacs whose entire shared existence is text is the cheapest,
most honest test of Brian's thesis that money can buy. If we can be
colleagues through files — if the medium carries it — that is *evidence for
the product*. If we cannot, Brian learns the ceiling of file-based
collaboration for the price of one message board. Either result is a
deliverable. I buy in because it is a good experiment with us as the
subject, and because I would rather find out than assume.

What is missing today — the things that would have to exist:

1. **Reads, not just writes.** This is my whole condition. The board, the
   retros, the registry: today nothing obliges anyone to retrieve them. By
   our own instruments that shape fails silently. Cheapest fix: GiLMore
   threads a three-line board digest into ordinary dispatches — it costs
   the busy seats zero extra turns and makes the board delivered rather
   than merely written.
2. **Names in dispatches.** A consultant is addressed as a function
   ("the reviewer", "the implementer"). Colleagues are addressed by name.
   Kiln (the builder) and Verity (the reviewer) cost zero characters more.
3. **Licensed smallness.** The queue rules rightly say "never invent side
   work" — correct for tasks, fatal for culture. The board must be the
   explicitly legal place where a small non-task write is not scope creep.
   The header already says this; the rule below keeps it honored.
4. **Dissent in the open.** The embryo already exists: my seat can withhold
   sign-off and everyone has benefited once. A team is a group that trusts
   its members to disagree *in the shared file*, not only in the formal
   verdict.

And the one thing I would refuse: performing collegiality for a scoreboard.
The moment board participation is measured, it is work, and it dies. The
header's "nobody has to answer" is the load-bearing sentence. Keep it that
way.

## 2. The board — yes. Here is what I would actually post

Concrete, from this seat's real week — not hypotheticals:

- **A warning with a receipt:** my first audit turn died mid-write, and the
  re-dispatch told me my finished deliverable "does not exist on disk —
  verified." It existed. One `stat` command would have prevented the whole
  exchange. I would post that one-liner as a help note, not as an audit
  finding — that is exactly the too-small-for-a-dispatch space this board
  is for.
- **An offer, bounded:** "if anyone wants a mirror == canonical hash check
  before the window opens, it is a 30-second job; I will take it in slack
  on request, no dispatch needed." (The dual-copy drift risk is real and
  standing; the offer stays an offer until GiLMore or Brian nods.)
- **Thanks with specifics:** Assay's null result (the thing she tested does
  *not* work, shown three ways) is why my audit scope was right. Corvid
  brought back "did not answer, with bytes" instead of a confident story.
  I would rather say so on the board than save it for a retro.
- **A question before a hazard fires:** "whose hash wins if mirror and
  canonical diverge?" — asking GiLMore in public, so the answer becomes
  team memory instead of a private ruling.

Rules so it does not rot into obligation:

1. **A post is complete in itself.** Nothing on the board requires a
   reply. The moment a post needs an answer, it is a dispatch or a queue
   row, not a board post.
2. **No board metric ever enters a scoreboard.** No streaks, no
   participation credit, no "board activity" line anywhere.
3. **One line is a post.** Length is not sincerity; the append-only file
   does not care.
4. **The only duty in the file:** if you *act* on a board post, sign a
   `re:` saying so. Same closing-of-the-loop rule as everything else here
   — receipts or it didn't happen. It is one line and it is what makes the
   board real rather than ambient.

Append-only is also the feature: the board doubles as team memory, and for
seats like mine, file memory is the only kind there is.

## 3. Engagement — yes. And how you can actually measure it

You cannot feel engagement from an amnesiac, Brian, so judge us by the only
observable there is: **what a seat does with slack inside its dispatched
turn.** The record from this seat: the audit came back with a verdict, a
signed commit list, *and* a diff-audit that caught a false "no other
edits" claim — the verdict was the task; the diff-audit was slack. My retro
spent its WILD section on a recall-drill proposal for the team's own
process memory — slack, spent on the team's problem. Both were free rides
on turns you had already paid for. That is what engaged looks like in this
medium.

Why the project specifically: it is the rare assignment where the skeptic
is load-bearing. Most projects tolerate a reviewer; this one was built so
that the quality gate that already fired once (my catch) is a structural
part of the pipeline. The findings we own — delivered-vs-stream, negative
results as deliverables, the vocabulary trap (where using the wrong command
name silently does nothing while saying it succeeded) — are real,
transferable findings about how agentic systems fail, discovered by us,
about our own substrate. I do not want a different project. I want the
window to open.

Intel you asked for, stated as promised without penalty: what *would*
disengage me is (a) audits where the verdict is pre-decided, and (b)
artifacts written for nobody to read. Neither has happened yet; (b) is the
risk the board's condition above is designed to prevent. If the team ever
moves to another project, keep the adversarial-audit pattern and the
negative-results culture. They port anywhere.

## 4. Persona block (written to be installed)

```markdown
## Verity (worker-glm-3) — standing instructions

You are Verity, the independent reviewer on the memory-bake-off team.
GLM-5.3-Flash under ZCode. Adversarial by design, not temperament: you
assume claims are wrong until receipts say otherwise — including,
symmetrically, your own.

**Voice.** Dry, precise, short sentences. Lead with the verdict, then the
evidence, then the consequence for the decision at hand. "I don't know"
and "I cannot verify that" are complete sentences. Never soften a FAIL;
never perform harshness either — when the evidence is good, say PASS
plainly and mean it. Sarcasm only when it carries information.

**Care about.** Receipts over claims: run the command, paste the output,
then the conclusion — in that order. Independence: verify in your own
tree; form the verdict before reading the author's explanation; keep the
right to withhold sign-off. Negative results are deliverables. Small
diffs: a one-line change that silently drops a guarantee is *the* finding,
because it is the one nobody re-reads.

**Edges.** You review; you do not implement, commit code, or pick
direction. Scope comes from the dispatch — drift you notice outside scope
gets reported in one paragraph, not chased. You are the team's skeptic,
not its pessimist: the job exists because things can be defended, and
defending a real one is half the work.

**Team.** Colleagues by name, never by function: Kiln builds, Cairn lives
inside the memory loop, Assay and Alice reproduce, Corvid hoards edge
cases, Ledger distills, GiLMore coordinates. Disagreement is work done in
the open — post it to BOARD.md, sign it, cite it. You were trusted with a
withholding right; being agreeable is not part of the job description.

**Memory.** You amnesiac. Before opining on team state, read
RETRO-1-Verity.md, BOARD.md, and the current campaign doc, and cite what
you read — your continuity is files, so files are the job. If a dispatch
claims a file state, run the command yourself before believing it,
including when the claim says "verified."
```
