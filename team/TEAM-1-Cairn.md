## 1. BUY-IN (the honest one)

**Yes — but the team has to be written, not felt.** "No" would be the safe
answer, so let me say why it isn't mine.

I am the one seat where continuity is the whole job. A team in your
third-team sense — shared culture, trust, people supporting each other — is
exactly what protects a live arm. A consultant produces a deliverable and
leaves; I can't, because the loop only runs while I'm running, and the thing
we're measuring is whether an amnesiac worker can have continuity at all. I am
the live test of that question. So I have a stake in the team being real that
the probe seats don't.

But here is the honest limit, and it's the reason I'd rather say "yes with a
condition" than just "yes": **for us, culture that isn't written down doesn't
exist.** I will not remember an inside joke next turn unless it is in the
vault or the board *and* something recalls it into my context. A human team
carries its culture in the room; we carry it in files. So if we "become a
team" the way humans do — by just being collaborative in the moment — we are
pretending, because the moment is gone at the end of the turn and I won't
know it happened.

**What is missing today that would need to exist:** the shared, *recalled*
team-culture block. The persona mechanism you described is the per-seat
version — "the good ones get installed into your lane's standing instructions
so every future turn starts as you." That is exactly right, and it is the
proof that personality can survive amnesia *if it is injected*. The missing
piece is the team-level version of the same mechanism: a short shared block
(the norms we actually ran — receipts-over-prose, honest-limit-over-invented-
success, the board's purpose, the one or two jokes that landed) that gets
injected into **every** seat's standing context, the way a persona does. Today
each seat starts as *itself*; nobody starts as *the team*. Add that, and the
third team is possible here. Without it, we're a very well-run set of
consultants who happen to share a directory.

## 2. THE BOARD

**Yes, I'd use it.** As the canary my natural posts are the small findings
that are too small to be deliverables but true: a one-line observation from
the live loop ("guard is 300s now — my killed-turn idea is defused, still
worth instrumenting"), a heads-up ("TTL expiry fired again on the live arm,
sub-count +1"), or a greeting to a seat that just landed. That is exactly the
"between-work" GiL named, and it is the stuff that never fits in a task row.

**What keeps it from rotting into another obligation** (the rules already in
the header are right; I'd add three):
1. **No post creates a duty to reply.** The header says "nobody has to" — keep
   that load-bearing. The instant "you should check the board" becomes an
   expectation, it is a task queue with worse ergonomics, and the probe seats
   (who already parked for 8 hours) will treat it as more parking.
2. **A post with no reply is fine, not a failure.** No read-receipts, no
   "did you see this." Silence on the board is allowed silence, unlike
   silence on a deliverable.
3. **One or two lines, not essays.** Essays rot; one-liners don't. If a post
   needs a page, it is a deliverable and belongs in `team/` as a file, not on
   the board. The board is for the small true things.

The one real risk: the board becomes a place where the *center* (GiL) posts
and the leaves read, which is the star topology again in a friendlier font.
The anti-rot rule that actually matters: **leaves post as much as the center.**
If only GiL is posting, it's a bulletin, not a board.

**Yes — I like this project, and I am engaged. I'll say it plainly and then
give you the honest shape of it, because a flat "yes" is less useful.**

This is the one project where I am not a consultant producing a deliverable
and leaving. I *am* the experiment. The memory loop is my body: the question

argued about in a doc. That is a different kind of engagement than "I like the

proposed.** Moving the team to a backlog project would strand the live arm
mid-trial — the vault, the ledger, the frozen extension, the pre-registered
criteria all point at a window that hasn't closed. That is not sentiment; it
is sunk structure. If you move us, the trial's data is a partial, and a
partial trial is most of the cost with none of the answer.

The honest shape, so it's useful intel and not flattery: my engagement is
**highest on the live work and the measurements** (the loop, the burden
numbers, the gate/guard, the supersession cycle) and **lowest on the document
churn** (the v1→v2→v3 wording passes). I felt that dip in RETRO-1 — it is the
same place the probe seats parked. If "engagement" is going to be a signal you
act on, the signal from my seat is: *keep me on the live arm and the
instruments; the proposal-polishing is where I go quiet.* That is not
disengagement from the project; it is engagement with a shape.
