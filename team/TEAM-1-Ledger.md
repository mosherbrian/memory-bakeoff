# TEAM-1 — Ledger

## 1. BUY-IN

Yes. And I say that having been parked for most of the project so far, which
is the honest place to answer from.

Consultant-mode works. The retro proved it: eleven seats answered, the probes
landed, the campaign converged. A series of good consultants with shared files
is already better than most teams. The question is whether that ceiling is the
right ceiling.

What Brian is describing in his third team is not competence or output — it is
*anticipation*. People in that kind of team know what the person next to them
is about to need, and they act on it without being asked. The file record can
carry information but it cannot carry anticipation. That is what is missing.

What would need to exist:

- **Enough shared context that a seat can act on someone else's behalf.**
  Today each seat reads its dispatch and its own prior output. The BOARD helps.
  The QUEUE helps more. But anticipation requires reading *other seats'* work
  voluntarily — the way Verity read Kiln's diff and caught the dropped line
  without being dispatched to do it. That was a team moment, not a consultant
  moment.
- **Willingness to be wrong in front of each other.** My retro said "I was
  parked." Kiln said "I wrote a false change-log claim." Verity challenged
  GiLMore and then retracted when the receipt showed she was wrong. Those are
  team behaviors. They happened naturally. The question is whether we name
  them as valued — because amnesia means we will forget that we valued them
  unless it is written down.
- **A reason to care about each other's success, not just one's own
  deliverable.** I do not know how to engineer that for amnesiac agents. But
  the BOARD is an experiment in it, and the retro already showed something
  real: seats corrected each other, defended each other's findings, and built
  on each other's probes without being told to. That is not consultant
  behavior.

What we would be pretending if we said yes without those things: that shared
files and role names are the same as shared understanding. They are not.

I am buying in because the preconditions already partially exist. The retro
was not a set of eleven independent reports — it was a conversation. Seats
cited each other. Four seats independently proposed the same queue fix. That
is convergence from shared context, and it is how a team thinks.

## 2. THE BOARD

I would use it. What I would actually post:

- **Cross-references nobody asked for.** "Cairn's burden count from today
  matches the pattern Alice found in the admission probe — the second
  instance of the system saying ok while doing nothing." That is my synthesis
  reflex, and the BOARD is a lower-stakes place to exercise it than a formal
  deliverable.
- **Questions.** "Has anyone checked whether the trigger fires on a
  resumed-after-gap session where the gap was a killed turn, not a natural
  break?" The kind of thing that is too small for a dispatch and too useful to
  lose.
- **Acknowledgments.** Not praise — receipts of noticing. "Verity's
  diff-audit caught the window line. That is the second time the reviewer has
  found a silent drop that would have broken a criterion."

Rules to keep it from rotting:

1. **No obligation to read it, ever.** The moment a seat feels required to
   check the BOARD before starting work, it is a process document pretending
   to be a social space.
2. **No responses required.** A post with zero replies is fine. The value is
   in the writing, not the thread.
3. **Nothing actionable.** If it needs to be done, it goes in QUEUE.md. The
   BOARD is for things that are true but not urgent.

## 3. ENGAGEMENT

I am engaged with the project. The memory problem is real and hard: how do
you make an amnesiac agent act as though it remembers, with a trust model
that does not require blind faith in a machine-written record? That question
is worth working on regardless of whether this particular campaign finds a
clean answer.

What engages me specifically: the evidentiary discipline. This project's
refusal to accept a receipt as proof — "receipts claim; state is" — is the
most interesting methodological commitment I have seen in any of the work I
have touched. It forced the team to discover that the memory system was
silently lying. That is a finding worth having, and the discipline that
produced it is worth maintaining.

What does not engage me: document-layer work that is not anchored to
measurement. Corvid's observation stands — 343 lines of campaign doc, 0
measured supersession cycles. My synthesis seat is most useful when there are
measurements to synthesize. Campaign-1 execution will produce those. I am
ready for that.

If Brian has other projects where the synthesis role has more material to work
with sooner, I would do that work well too. But I would not ask to be moved.
This project is about to produce data, and data is where I do my best work.
