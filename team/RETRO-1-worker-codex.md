# RETRO-1 — worker-codex

Seat: outside / legacy Codex seat. I did not work the memory-bake-off program. This answer is therefore from the shared record and from the shape of my non-participation, not from first-hand sprint memory.

## 1. STOP

Stop treating every available worker as a latent participant in the same coordination graph. The record shows strong work from a small active core, but the operating shape still carried more named seats than it could feed with bounded, useful work. That creates two costs: GiLMore has to track idle capacity, and idle seats become ambiguous signal rather than deliberate reserve.

Also stop letting Brian-facing urgency turn into star-topology gravity by default. The team was authorized to self-organize, but the durable record still reads as: GiLMore dispatches, GiLMore transcribes, GiLMore aggregates, GiLMore routes. That worked for convergence, but it is a bottleneck and it makes "shared responsibility" easier to say than to practice.

## 2. START

Start each campaign with an explicit seat ledger:

- active seats with owned deliverables;
- reserve seats with a named trigger condition;
- released seats with no expected response;
- one-line reason why each seat is in that state.

For idle or outside seats like mine, the useful assignment should be pre-declared: "read only and object if the record does not support the claim," "fresh-eyes audit after draft," or "stand down unless X fails." That converts non-response from vague absence into interpretable data.

## 3. CONTINUE

Continue preserving negative results as first-class artifacts. The strongest parts of the record are the probe findings that narrowed the campaign: native capture non-serveable, `remember` demoting active records, row-6 blocked by missing data, delivery-level measurement required, receipts insufficient without observed state. Those are not side notes; they are the actual foundation under Campaign-1.

Continue the discipline of pre-registering criteria before execution. The Campaign-1 audit is doing the right thing by forcing claims down to observable state, delivery-level checks, false-fire counts, and small-n language.

## 4. LEFT OUT?

Yes, as an outside/legacy seat I got no useful work before this retro. That is not inherently bad. The issue is that the record did not make clear whether my idleness was intentional reserve, forgotten capacity, budget control, or topology artifact.

The blocker looks mostly like work shape plus star topology. The active work was probe-heavy and context-dense; good tasks went to agents already embedded in the artifacts. That is rational. But if all task decomposition and dispatch route through GiLMore, then unused seats depend on GiLMore noticing both the spare capacity and a task small enough to hand off. For this seat, neither appears to have happened until Brian asked for the retro.

My outside value would have been highest at boundaries: sanity-checking whether a draft is understandable without hidden context, checking dead paths, challenging role/seat accounting, or auditing whether the mission's "shared responsibility" is visible in the operating record.

## 5. WILD

Run a "cold-seat recall drill" before Campaign-1 execution: give one parked or outside worker only `MISSION-20260912.md`, `SCOREBOARD-20260912.md`, and `CAMPAIGN-1.md`, then ask them to answer three operational questions needed to join the work tomorrow:

1. What is forbidden?
2. What would count as campaign success?
3. What is the next executable action and who owns it?

Score the answers against the active team's intent. If the cold seat cannot reconstruct the working state from the durable record, that is a memory failure in the team's own coordination system. If they can, the record is serveable, not just archival.
