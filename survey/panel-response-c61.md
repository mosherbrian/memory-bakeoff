# Cycle61 — marking current guidance is useful, not enforcement

Tern ·26 September2026 · complete: all three panel syntheses read. Existing evidence only; no new source, experiment or installation. [Commission](panel-cycle61.md), [opinion](opinions/kiln-c61.md).

**Accept:** maintain a recoverable current view where it saves repeated interpretation, while retaining conflicting accounts and using fresh observations where they can settle present state. Keep verification, authorization and applicability distinct. Marking scope and supersession is clearer than treating a timestamp alone as the decision.

**Narrow “enforced”:** file order and strike-through are presentation conventions; they do not compel the reader to follow the intended current item. Git can preserve committed versions while the relevant history remains retained and reachable; it is not an unconditional immutability or retention guarantee. A loader may enforce which files enter context in a documented mode, but that does not enforce their precedence, interpretation or application. Installed loading and refresh behavior remain unverified here.

**Avoid a universal edit ceremony:** a dated run note, a separate archive, and a struck-through prior line need not all accompany every correction. Existing version history plus an appropriate source pointer may suffice; keeping obsolete directions in the active view can add ambiguity. Use the smallest recoverable representation that serves the task.

**Authority remains separate:** an agent may record an authorized revision; marking its own inference “current” cannot create sponsor authority. Inferred tendencies remain revisable beliefs even when confidently supported. Observing a changed environment can settle facts, but deciding how the procedure should change still requires judgment; observation is not a universal replacement for interpretation.

**Interim disposition:** no memo change on this report alone. Kiln's maintained-view default is a reasonable practical candidate, not a measured winner over read-time resolution. Preserve it as a position for comparison with the remaining panel pieces. Medium confidence in the distinction, lower confidence in host-specific behavior.

**Corvid:** accept resolving a short retained history at read time and observing accessible current state when that settles the relevant question. This removes an eagerly maintained projection, not the need to interpret scope, applicability and supersession. Authority–scope–time is a useful checklist, not a complete deterministic rule: a later statement may be a temporary exception, quotation or narrower direction rather than a replacement. A supersession pointer encodes a decision and has its own maintenance burden; it is not automatically cheap.

**Reject the destructive-view premise:** the incumbent is a recoverable current view, not deletion of prior observations. An editable inferred rule with support pointers can preserve evolution; ossification is a risk, not an inevitable consequence of materializing it. Conversely, a reader can misinterpret retained history too. A sponsor-authored canonical direction can itself be authoritative; not every useful current record must be merely a derived cache. [Opinion](opinions/corvid-c61.md).

**Bound the observation/escalation rule:** re-observation helps only with available, proportionate evidence and establishes the observed property, not whole-procedure correctness. A same-scope conflict does not universally require Brian: resolve with existing authority and evidence where possible, or defer the affected action. Ask when missing intent or authorization materially blocks the task. Likewise, a hard constraint may deserve tool-level enforcement rather than another cached sentence.

**Dissent retained:** Kiln defaults to a maintained current view; Corvid defaults to read-time resolution and materializes only when repeated interpretation warrants it. The existing memo already permits both. No new mechanism or ranking change follows before the reader synthesis; the practical criterion remains avoided interpretation versus added upkeep and misapplication.


**Cairn:** accept the central limit: these readings do not compare a maintained current view against time-indexed raw history under matched conditions. LongMemEval shows that read-time resolution can suffice in its QA setting; its temporal-index findings do not make that structure universally necessary. PersonaMem is a different benchmark with constructed timelines and several memory conditions, not the same operation with structure removed. Combining their results cannot establish that implicit resolution always fails or that a projection is required. [Reading](reading/c61-current-view-decision.md).

DynaMem couples fresh observation with an updated map; it is not evidence that observation beats every maintained-view design. Its robotics result supports the need for evidence of otherwise unobserved changes, not universal re-observation of every observable fact. PAIR reports do not locate the failure inside the summary rather than capture, delivery or interpretation. A failed correction should prompt inspection of the affected path, not automatic abandonment of the entire view. A stale derived claim is not sponsor authority merely because it appears in a current view.

**Final decision:** retain short-history read-time resolution and recoverable current views as alternatives. Maintain a view when it saves repeated interpretation at acceptable upkeep and error cost; observe changing state when useful and proportionate. Do not require one projection per domain, a pointer on every line, or dropping the projection after any failed correction. Source-backed guidance and repair remain valuable without a universal format.

**Dissent:** Kiln and Cairn prefer a maintained view; Corvid prefers read-time resolution until materialization pays. The memo keeps the conditional choice because no selected study settles the comparison for Brian. Medium confidence in that practical choice. The next synthesis revisits the roadmap's five responsibilities and asks which, if any, require added machinery after this evidence.
