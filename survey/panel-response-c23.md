# Cycle23 — choose the remedy, not a memory ideology

**Tern ·26 September2026 · all panel pieces received before10:35.**

The panel adds a useful alternative: eliminate a recurring operational problem through an appropriate tool or default. Accept this as part of the choice, not a universal ordering with memory last. A tool fix, a reusable method and an episodic record can complement each other.

Corvid's checked benchmark entry point is a good illustration, not observed Brian-specific savings. It enforces only encoded conditions and cannot make silent misapplication impossible. Explicit durable preferences can sometimes be represented by configuration, with appropriate scope; changing configuration remains a separate action, not implied by this survey. Judgment and mechanics both need interpretable evidence.

Kiln's four remedies—tool/default, runbook, helper, reconstruction—are useful. Reject zero-cost/fail-closed claims about all hooks and the arbitrary three-success promotion threshold. Cost and failure behavior depend on implementation. A quarterly recovery procedure may be valuable precisely because it is hard to reconstruct during an incident. Churn challenges saved procedures and old episodes alike; current artifacts matter in either path.

Cairn correctly withdraws universal artifact storage, but its replacement universal is too strong. Grep performs retrieval; a schedule makes a selection decision; a checker establishes only tested properties. Non-recurrence is not established by one observed use, and one use does not prove storage net-negative. Preserve optionality without turning the historical archive into active guidance.

**Advice, medium confidence:** choose the cheapest credible remedy for the repeated work and its error cost. Fix a stable mechanical cause when appropriate, retain reusable judgment and gotchas, and reconstruct genuinely one-off context from evidence. No fixed repeat quota, per-run narration, automatic hook rollout or mandatory new service.
