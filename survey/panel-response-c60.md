# Cycle60 — acquisition should earn its cost, without a fixed use count

Tern ·26 September2026 · complete: all three panel syntheses read. Existing evidence only; no new source, experiment or deployment. [Commission](panel-cycle60.md), [opinion](opinions/kiln-c60.md).

**Accept:** use existing artifacts, preserve the originating example and useful scope, and keep reconstruction available when reuse fails. Omit dedicated acquisition machinery until a concrete benefit warrants its costs. This is a practical default, not a measured claim that it covers all Brian's work.

**Reject the fixed threshold:** “second need—never the first” has no supporting comparison in these readings. A helper may be the cheapest way to finish the first task, or anticipated reuse may justify early construction. Conversely, a second occurrence does not establish enough benefit to repay upkeep. Recurrence is evidence for the decision, not a universal count rule.

**No mandatory medium ladder:** worked example, parameterized procedure and executable helper are alternatives chosen for the task, not successive promotion stages. Existing tests establish their tested properties; shells and repos do not automatically supply benchmark-quality labels. Useful naming and versioned artifacts do not require a registry or standard doc format.

**Failure does not mandate demotion:** inspect whether the fault lies in the artifact, its selection, arguments, current environment or the checker. Repairing a small defect in versioned code can be cheaper than reconstructing the procedure. Preserve needed evidence; demote or retire guidance when justified, not after every failed use. The earlier worked arrangement did not establish a universal log-first rule.

**Memo disposition:** no change on this report alone. Keep selective acquisition and recovery; decline the second-use threshold, mandatory medium progression, and prohibition on in-place repair. This was the initial disposition before the other reports arrived. Medium confidence in this practical judgment; no measured break-even for Brian.


**Corvid:** accept on-demand authoring as the practical starting point when the current task benefits from a helper. This clarifies an option already allowed by the baseline; it is not a demonstrated cross-study winner. It avoids speculative batch construction but still requires deciding what to create, retain and maintain. Reconstruction can remain economical even for repeated tasks when reuse is sparse, churn high or the reusable core tiny. Stable distribution alone neither proves batch acquisition will repay its cost nor is the only condition under which it could. Lack of a cheap comprehensive checker does not automatically make reconstruction more reliable; choose proportionate checks and preserve uncertainty. The original-case regression is useful where available, not a universal additional step. [Opinion](opinions/corvid-c60.md).

**Interim memo change:** prefer solving the actual task first and retaining a useful helper created along the way; avoid speculative toolset construction without a concrete anticipated payoff. Keep bounded practice and batch acquisition available. Medium confidence as applied judgment; equal-total-cost comparisons and Brian-specific break-even remain unknown. The final response below incorporates the reader synthesis.


**Cairn:** keep bounded practice as a serious alternative when an actual capability gap warrants it. The CRAFT executor comparison shows heterogeneous gains, not an isolated causal law about the author–executor capacity gap; it cannot place Brian's local models on a proven high-gain side. Poor open-source pilots concern tool-use competence, not proof of author quality. FRIDAY's interface explanation remains a plausible, unisolated moderator; positive GAIA gains do not justify “store nothing” whenever a model can improvise. CRAFT's abstraction control supports generalizing code in that setting, not abandoning raw episodes. Name matching is one retrieval cue, not the sole handle; its ablation losses include exactly 6.6 points. [Reading](reading/c60-acquisition-decision.md).

**Limits on the proposed rule:** requiring an original-case check for every retained entry is still a per-record requirement; apply it where useful, without importing name/arity dedup into prose guidance. Batch construction is not restricted by these results to independently labeled domains—CRAFT's VQA construction itself uses weaker supervision. Sponsor authority is a different category from empirical verification, not merely a weaker admission gate. Absence of a matched total-cost comparison in these readings does not establish that no break-even evidence exists elsewhere.

**Final choice and dissent:** start with the actual task; create and retain useful artifacts selectively. Cairn favors a short practice phase for demonstrated capability gaps; Corvid favors on-demand creation. Both remain credible options, with practice or batch construction justified by a concrete expected benefit. No study here establishes their relative total cost for Brian, successful local transfer, or reliability after software prerequisites change. This warrants medium confidence, not a universal acquisition policy. Next examine when a current-guidance decision belongs in read-time interpretation versus a maintained projection, using existing evidence only.
