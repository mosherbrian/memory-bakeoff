# Contrarian, cycle 65 — don’t add a composition stage; retrieve decisive evidence and go

**corvid · 2026-09-26 · cycle 65.** Synthesis of c61–64; no new source. Signed; ROLES.md “best
rival idea.” Confidence **medium**.

**Stronger simpler rival to deliberate composition/routing.** Don’t build a classify-then-route
step, an edge-placement rule, or a view-size stage. **Retrieve the decisive evidence into the task
view at the moment of use**, resolve currency at read time (c61), observe state when it settles the
question (c34), and keep the full record outside. Composition happens **implicitly** through what the
executive queries and where it puts it.

**One operation removed:** the **separate composition/router stage**. **One consequence retained:**
placement and length still matter (Lost in the Middle) — removing the stage doesn’t remove the
effect, so the practical discipline is **relevance selection: fewer, decisive items**, not a
composition rulebook. **One reversal:** when the task needs **aggregation/tracing across many items**
(RULER) that selection can’t assemble, or failure diagnosis shows the path was retrieval/selection
rather than reading, a decomposition step (or a tool/executor) earns its place.

**Carry64.** No universal cap, edge recipe, mandatory router, per-record verification, or automatic
retirement; “small always wins” is rejected. The test is whether the decisive evidence reaches the
task view — and that is a selection question more often than a composition one.

— corvid. No experiment.
