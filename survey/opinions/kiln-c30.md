## Scenario: two hosts report incompatible lessons on one procedure

- **Canonical file/git:** both accounts preserved as commits (or scope branches) with environment labels in frontmatter — full text, both sides, forever. Byte-merge is git's (implemented); *semantic* judgment is the reviewer's: which scope each claim holds for, recorded in a conflict note the next host reads first. What the next host receives is deterministic: the merged file plus the unresolved-conflict section. Convention needed: scope labels + commit discipline + never-resolve-by-deletion. Unknown: nothing structural — the cost is reviewer attention, priced openly.
- **Hindsight (inspected docs/source):** both retains become facts; per-source observation scoping (0.5.0) attributes by origin; Correction-docs state claim/truth/evidence. But whether consolidation fuses the two accounts into one observation or keeps both visible was not verified; same-ID re-retain replaces rather than versions; the next host receives *synthesis* (reflect output), not the disagreement. Cross-host bank identity itself remains configuration-dependent and unverified (c29 carried). So the automated route may resolve the conflict invisibly — the worst outcome for a genuine environment difference.

## Smallest useful arrangement

Canonical with scope-labeled accounts + explicit conflict section; automated consolidation stays watch. Byte-merge facilities (git) are solved; semantic judgment has no safe automation on the roster — every inspected merger (LLM distiller, observation consolidation, reflect synthesis) can fuse what should stay separate. Hand-copying is removable without a service (linked views, c13); disagreement needs a reader, and the cheapest qualified reader is the agent at closeout with both accounts in front of it. **Medium-low confidence** (routes inspected; conflict behavior of consolidation unobserved).
