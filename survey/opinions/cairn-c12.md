Signed opinion from my sweep only (c1–c11); no new source commissioned.

**The finding that most changes the comparison: Capture's theorem plus its measured
ask-budget.** Recency-and-provenance rules are provably bound-broken — an adversary or a
legitimate revision can look identical on those features. That kills **B** as a default
(automatic integrated memory must either accept contradictions or reject them, and both failure
directions are measured) and it kills **A** as a *preference* mechanism (files give recency and
provenance and nothing else — every change falls back on Brian restating it, his cost #2). What
survives measurement is a hybrid that looks like **A+C**: an external, scope-and-timescale-stamped
record (A's artifact) that a learned proposer reads over, with the human asked only on the
uncertain few (~1 per 12 in Capture's budget; our lane's 59/88 expired confirms is what an
unbudgeted gate measures like). Titans-style parametric memory can coexist underneath a retained
external log; the corollary binds the record, not the runtime.

**Largest unmeasured user cost: Brian's attention spent managing memory** — confirming writes,
noticing stale or mis-scoped recall, undoing silent wrong UPDATEs (outcome-gated correction
never touches what no question revisits). No paper measures it; our expired-confirm count is the
only number on the table, and it already dominates the confirm channel.

Confidence: medium-high on the theorem's direction, medium on extrapolating the 1/12 ask-rate to
Brian's traffic.
