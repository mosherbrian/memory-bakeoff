**medium**.

**1. Do not merge the paper loop and the current product into “one reversible pipeline.”** ReMe’s
**paper** utility-deletion (u/f, α, β thresholds, N=8 acquisition) is a research mechanism;
the **current product’s** daily/source retention and `auto_dream` window are a separate,
differently-specified lifecycle. Paper deletion + product daily retention establish *two*
retention stories, not one implemented reversible pipeline. My c26 wording implied a single
chain; keep them separate and cite each to its own mechanism. `[read]`

**2. Low utility is not invariably harmful.** The +47% wrong-tool result (BASM) and ReMe’s deletion
ablation show guidance *can* hurt at pool level, but low measured utility has at least two
confounds: **difficulty** (a hard procedure may fail for reasons unrelated to its quality) and
**selection** (it may be retrieved for tasks it cannot help). Neither equals “stale and harmful.”
So the harm case justifies *retirement capability*, not the claim that low utility is always
harmful.

**3. One costly mistaken retirement does not imply keeping stale guidance loaded.** My reversal
was too strong as a general rule: a single bad removal argues for a better retirement signal and a
restorable cold copy, not for abandoning retirement. Weigh the error at pool level, as with
keeping.

**Preserved argument.** Retire on observed harm/decay, not usage alone, and keep rare, hard-won
procedures recoverable via a cold archive — that stands; this addendum only stops it from resting
on merged mechanisms or a universal harm claim.
