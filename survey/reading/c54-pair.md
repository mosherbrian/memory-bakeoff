## Corrections given, counted, and reported lost: first field evidence on correction uptake (2609.16344v2, §4.2.2, §5, §6.2–6.4) `[read]`

**Participants and exposure: real.** 24 recruited, 5 withdrew → **19 completed a 14-day field
deployment, 1,093 sessions** (3 daily touchpoints, ≤5-min conversations), paid up to $80 by
completed days, IRB, explicit not-a-clinical-tool framing. **No comparator** for the deployment
(single-system; baselines exist only in the emotion-estimation sub-study). Repeated-measure
analysis is honest: mixed-effects day trends, Wilcoxon with Holm/FDR, nulls reported (session
frequency flat; feasibility flat).

**Memory: bundled, not isolated.** A nightly ≤150-word summary (recurring concerns,
estimate-vs-self-report gaps, **user corrections**, strategy outcomes) feeds later calls. No
memory-off arm — its contribution is asserted, never tested.

**Corrections actually counted:** correction text in **4.0% of sessions** (offered only when the
understanding rating was ≤4); **44 texts, 37 system-attributable = 3.4% of sessions** — emotion
misreads 15, context confusion 8, **memory failures 8**, strategy mismatch 6. Corrected sessions
showed *lower* within-session valence gain (paired Wilcoxon, 12 participants). Uptake of guidance
probed in evening sessions: 44 responses, LLM-assisted then manually reviewed categories.

**What longitudinal adds over c53's staged edits and c52's trace audit: persistence failure,
observed behaviorally.** Six of 19 reported feedback incorporated; **nine described corrections
that did not persist or stopped correcting the system** — P18 "correct it two or three times,"
P4 understood in the moment "but later it would forget again," P2 a three-day-old event treated
as current even after correction; 12 of 19 reported forgotten context; P15's occasional
activities hardened into recurring assumptions. **Users stop correcting when corrections do not
stick** — the field-scale cousin of c14's confidence-silencing. Their design implication matches
this panel's position: entries with source and dates, **explicit statements distinguished from
system interpretations**, edits propagated to summaries, and **evaluation that checks whether
later conversations actually incorporate corrections**.

**Limits:** emotion support ≠ coding workflow; outcomes self-reported; no memory isolation;
corrections are low-base-rate; attrition 5/24.

**Verdict: the corpus's strongest real-repeated-use evidence — corrections are rare (gated
count), and participants report them not persisting or cease giving them (combined interview
category; reports, not a logged correction-to-later-action audit; incidence bounded by the
gate; correction time unpriced).** For Brian: acceptance testing for a preference store should
be "does a later conversation apply the correction," and reported non-persistence or ceasing
correcting (9/19, combined category) is the uptake signal to watch in his own lane. Confidence:
high on what was measured (explicit counts/tests), medium on qualitative persistence claims,
high that memory contribution is unisolated (searched).
