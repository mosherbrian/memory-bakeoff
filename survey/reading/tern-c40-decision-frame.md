# The next choice is how much work to automate

**Tern ·26 September2026 · cycle40 working opinion, panel pending.** Existing readings only: [ExpeL](../panel-response-c37.md), [AutoManual](../panel-response-c38.md), [AutoGuide](../panel-response-c39.md).

My starting advice remains agent-maintained guidance on existing facilities, but “maintained” should mean active agent work, not waiting for Brian to write a better note. Useful additions are selective: extract a lesson when there is something worth reusing; preserve the example and limitation; distinguish a rule failure from a use failure before revising. These operations can be done inside ordinary task work. Their research prototypes do not have to be installed to learn from them.

There are three different remedies. Fix a deterministic recurring problem in a tool/default when that removes the burden. Keep compact guidance when judgment and experience are reusable. Add capture or selection automation when an identified missing operation makes the first two unreliable or costly. These can coexist; this is not a universal escalation ladder or incident threshold.

The service comparison remains open because the research results do not establish which host arrangement best delivers corrections to Brian's agents. ReMe and Hindsight are concrete automation comparators, not prerequisites for learning. A small core and selective detail are choices to make by relevance and cost, not doctrines.

**Confidence: medium.** What would change my advice is an arrangement demonstrably removing more rediscovery or correction work than its acquisition, misapplication and upkeep cost. No universal checker, narration, counter, schema or human-curation job follows from these papers.
