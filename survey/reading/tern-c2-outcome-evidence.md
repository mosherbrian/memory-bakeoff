# Procedure reuse: outcome evidence is stronger, but conditional

**Tern · cycle 2 · 26 September 2026.** Primary methods and limitations read; no reproduction. These studies address different decisions and are not pooled.

| Source / reading depth | What changes the decision | Verdict and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [AFTER](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23127v1), §§2–3, limitations and splits | Workplace tasks have executable checks and train/validation/test partitions. Assigned skills are supplied directly, separating content quality from discovery. Reported gains and transfer failures make procedure content a credible intervention. | **Solid for the narrower content/transfer question; medium confidence in relevance.** It does not show that an agent will find the right skill, maintain it cheaply, or satisfy untested preferences. Aggregate “best” skill gains also are not a prospective universal selection policy. |
| [Skill-Evo4GUI](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04869v1), §§3–5 | An evolving library is compared with an empty one under matched execution settings. Repeated tasks and one run per condition/domain restrict causal and transfer claims; warm-up imbalance matters. Accepted revisions sometimes continue without task recovery. | **Useful longitudinal evidence, medium confidence.** Stronger relevance to repeated work than workflow QA, but no basis for promising automatic improvement or unlimited revision. |
| [Boundary-Aware Skill Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22339v1), §§4–5 and limitations | Explicit applicability, avoidance and recovery information accompanies procedures. Prompt-only and runtime-repair ablations behave differently across function calling and multi-step work. Small-model utility can remain below the memory-free baseline despite improved attack resistance. | **Promising mechanism, medium confidence.** A boundary field alone is not enforcement or a universal gain. Tests use fixed text-based settings; changing APIs and personal preferences remain transfer questions. |

**My position:** a skill earns its place by avoiding recurring discovery or mistakes, not by being elegant or executable. Keep the reusable part smaller than the changing environment: an investigation sequence can survive where hard-coded commands cannot. Store reasons not to use a procedure alongside the useful recipe. A failed attempt belongs in recoverable history, clearly labelled, rather than being deleted or recommended as success.

**Choice rule (Tern's heuristic, not a measured equation):** favor reuse when saved discovery and avoided mistakes exceed authoring, checking, maintenance, and added misapplication costs. Count reconstruction's costs too, including selecting an obsolete episode. Exact version equality is sometimes useful but not a universal requirement; a procedure can deliberately cover a supported version range or delegate an uncertain step to current documentation. Conversely, a matching version does not prove matching permissions, data or side effects.

**Effect on Q5/Q3:** stronger support for scoped reusable procedures, still no universal choice among prose, executable helpers and reconstruction. The missing joint evidence concerns discovery + applicability + completion + upkeep. Do not demand all of that before making a proportionate, reversible recommendation; do not claim it has been established.
