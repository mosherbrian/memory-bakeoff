# Representation changes where the hard decision happens

**Tern · 26 September 2026 · primary reading, no experiment.**

**SEAL** ([v2 methods/limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10943v2)) learns to generate adaptation data/directives, with downstream performance rewarding an outer policy and inner gradient updates changing weights. Its knowledge and simplified abstract-reasoning tasks establish an adaptation mechanism, not validated operational runbooks. Contrary to the panel’s untested-forgetting statement, §5 actually evaluates successive edits and finds declining prior-task performance; retention is not explicitly optimized. Weight adaptation removes the external document-fetch step, not the possibility of failing to produce learned information. “Compiler” is a useful analogy, not guaranteed faithful compilation. External training data, checkpoints and scoped adapters could retain provenance or reversibility; the experiments do not supply a complete correction/access protocol, but they do not prove such protocols impossible. **Watch, medium confidence in mechanism; low confidence in net value for Brian’s heterogeneous hosts.**

**MemStrata** ([v1 §3 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26511v1)) gives a concrete deterministic branch for a single mutable value under a normalized subject/relation key. Extraction still decides the key. The published extractor explicitly routes preferences/decisions out of that branch, including its Python-versus-Go preference example; the multi-value prompt does likewise. Thus Kiln’s preference-triple illustration is a possible schema design, not demonstrated behavior of this prompt. The paper limits its strong result to structured single-value cases and acknowledges poor extraction on messier statements. **Watch, medium confidence in the scoped mechanism, low in a preference/procedure winner.** My inference: correctness depends on encoding scope and whether the relation really permits only one current value; deterministic replacement cannot settle that semantics by itself. No foreign-system failure counts transferred.

**Titans** ([architecture description](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00663v1)) distinguishes a test-time neural store of contextual history from input-independent learned persistent parameters, coupled with short-window attention. Its long-context experiments do not establish a portable, cross-session preference store for Brian. **Medium confidence in taxonomy; current local feasibility unknown.** Keep architectural memory, per-input adaptation and durable user records as distinct capabilities rather than products on one leaderboard.

**Register effect:** scope and applicability remain decisions wherever the representation moves them. Deterministic transitions can improve reproducibility; learned representations can reduce explicit context. Neither automatically supplies correct scope, accessible evidence or cheap correction.

## StateMem follow-up

[StateMem §§4–6 and Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19652v1) separates LLM-proposed state units/supersessions from deterministic propagation of recheck flags. It also supplies an answer-time wrapper. The benchmark supplies explicit conflicts; broader conflict discovery is a different task. Crucial correction to the card: its answer pool is **hidden at probe time**; graders map free-form outputs to that pool. It therefore does test generation, with closed-pool scoring limits. The wrapper receives transcripts as well as retrieved chunks, and its small instruction increment is relative to a matched transcript-sized control—not proof of that tiny total overhead over retrieval-only use. Wrapper and full-method absolute scores also come from different evaluation mixes. **Watch, medium confidence:** candidate state discipline, not evidence that persistent state pays only beyond context limits. Current facts, derived consequences and retained historical values need distinct treatment.

## Memory-R1 follow-up

[Memory-R1 v1 §§3–4 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.19828v1) trains operation selection over an external bank and answer-time memory filtering. This is a learned controller over editable records, distinct from storing the facts only in weights. Its small QA training set is accompanied by generated temporal-bank training material; example count alone is not acquisition cost. The v1 evidence is conversational QA, with held-out dialogues, not procedural action benefit. Training changes the policy, but runtime constraints or a different controller can also alter how proposed operations are applied—my design inference, not a tested feature. **Medium confidence in mechanism; Brian-specific benefit unknown.** “No correction except retraining” is too absolute operationally. We do not pool version-specific performance claims or call the method a product-level failure because its paper omits a full audit/retention system.
