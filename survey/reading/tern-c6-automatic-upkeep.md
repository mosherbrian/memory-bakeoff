# Automatic upkeep: what is learned, what is merely rewarded?

**Tern · 26 September 2026 · primary-method reading, no experiments.**

**ReasoningBank — useful evidence for agent-authored learning, medium confidence in transfer.** [Methods and appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140v1) extract advice from self-judged successes and failures; the stored entry also retains the task and trajectory. The evaluated consolidation policy appends without pruning. Browser and software-task comparisons include success and interaction-step outcomes, while the separate MaTTS extension spends extra computation on experience generation/refinement. This supports automatically authored procedural advice over a blanket preference for manual runbooks. A noisy self-judge remains a limitation, and fewer interaction steps do not establish lower total cost after memory induction. The results do not resolve long-term invalidation under changing prerequisites.

**ReMe — a concrete refinement competitor, medium confidence.** [§§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10696v1) combine extraction, task-specific reuse and pool revision. Initial memories include comparative failure analysis; online admission favors successful trajectories, with bounded reflection/retrial for failures. Deletion uses a minimum retrieval count and historical success-associated utility. The evaluation separates initial acquisition from held-out tasks and distinguishes fixed from dynamically updated memory; its four-trial metrics must not be read as first-attempt reliability. Gains in this setup make automatic maintenance worth considering. My concern is credit assignment: an entry retrieved during a difficult failed task is not thereby a bad procedure, and success alongside an entry is not a controlled estimate of its contribution. The paper's heuristic does not establish that distinction.

**Sleep-time Compute — preparation, not demonstrated runbook maintenance.** [Methods and SWE case](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.13171v1) examine preprocessing known context before an unseen query. Predictability and reuse can amortize preparation. Crucially, the SWE endpoint is overlap between edited file sets and reference PR files, not executable correctness. Reported precision there means file-selection precision. This supports precomputation as an option, but does not demonstrate correct procedure retirement or preference reversal. Background work still has a cost; query-time savings and total savings are distinct.

**My recommendation:** agents should do routine capture and revision using available evidence; Brian should not become the memory librarian. Keep raw failures and prior versions recoverable while promoting selected lessons into current guidance. Reversible, attributable edits and selective checks are a better starting policy than either accepting every reflection or making the user approve every write. **Medium confidence.** This explicitly assigns maintenance to the agent; it does not select one automatic learning algorithm or authorize a deployment.
